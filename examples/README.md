# Worked example: fictional production reports

These files are human-written reference material. They are not results from a paid model evaluation.

1. Read [source-notes.md](source-notes.md): two note types cover the same fictional project.
2. Follow the repository README's free walkthrough to save them as input.
3. Compare [game-report.md](game-report.md) to the source facts.
4. Compare [clan-report.md](clan-report.md) to the reviewed game report.
5. For paid testing, generate your own drafts and use the acceptance checks below. Exact wording will vary.

## Acceptance checks

- Art is complete; QA is planned for 19 October and owned by Morgan.
- Internal review is planned for 22 October, conditional on QA findings.
- Alex's 16 October sound review is not sound approval.
- Sound approval timing, release date and certification approval remain unconfirmed.
- Morgan's audio regression depends on the sound decision.
- The team summary retains the full game name, open dependency and required decision.
- No fabricated completion percentages, certification, release dates or commercial claims appear.

An acceptable draft preserves those facts and unknowns; attractive wording alone is insufficient.
For conflicting notes, mark the conflict and ask for confirmation rather than choosing an answer.

## Small pilot, before commercial claims

Use permitted or fictional notes. Record time spent preparing inputs, correcting the draft and producing
the final report; compare against your existing manual process on similar work. Count omissions and
invented facts. A useful pilot needs a producer's review; passing automated tests does not establish
accuracy or justify selling autonomous reporting.
